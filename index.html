Selected Work02

AI Reliability in Financial Benchmarking

Validation · prototype

Before building an AI benchmarking workflow, I tested its riskiest step: whether a model could reliably identify the right peer companies. I ran a controlled experiment against trusted ground-truth sets across several sectors, scored agreement across 16 runs, and diagnosed that the worst failure was a criteria-definition mismatch rather than a capability gap. Writing the criteria into the prompt lifted agreement on the hardest case from near-zero to ~68%. I turned the finding into a hybrid architecture (deterministic screen, model selects and explains within it, human sign-off, deterministic Python for the financials) and built the prototype for the deterministic core.

Designed, ran, and presented to CTO · sponsored to build · code coming to GitHub

Audio Interpretability: Can a Model Hear a Wrong Note?

In progress

An interpretability project that started from playing the piano. I record two versions of a piece, one clean and one with a deliberate wrong note, and probe where inside an open audio model the two representations diverge — letting the layer-by-layer evidence, not a guess, locate where the model registers the error. A small study of whether, and where, a non-human system perceives a human mistake.

Learning mechanistic interpretability (forward hooks, probing, TransformerLens) to build it

AI Workflow Architecture for an Advisory Firm

Design · adopted

On my own initiative, I built a mental model of how AI should work at the firm — not task-level tools, but governed, workflow-level systems — including the distinction between agentic and AI-augmented workflows, where human checkpoints belong, and how client data stays compliant. I presented it to the CTO, drove consensus, and secured sponsorship to lead the build. Separately, a prompt-engineering framework I designed was adopted as a practice-wide standard.

Vision → consensus → sponsored build
